How I worked

Only about 3% of what I simulated qualified, so searching blindly would mostly find noise. Every signal started as a written thesis: who is on the other side of the trade, and why the price should correct. I kept parameters few, judged each signal in every test period rather than on one average, and charged it for turnover.

Four ideas, four different reasons prices go wrong

Each chart is the real cumulative backtest PnL from 2007 to 2021 on a $1M long/short book, drawn on the same scale.

Accounting

Firms that build assets beat firms that buy them

Accounting flatters the buyer: a deal puts goodwill on the balance sheet at the price paid, while R&D spent building assets is expensed and never shows up. Buyers look asset-rich, builders look less profitable, and the market does not fully correct for this.

Microstructure

A week of selling into the close bounces back

When a calm stock closes below its last-hour VWAP and near its daily low for a whole week, the likely cause is a large seller working an order into the close, not news. Once the order is done, the discount is recovered.

Over-reaction

Quiet stocks near a monthly low drift back

Among stocks with narrow daily ranges, a close near the bottom of the past month’s high–low range is usually temporary selling, and price drifts back toward the middle. Leaning toward firms that can service their debt keeps credit trouble out.

Anchoring

Dips in stocks near their one-year high recover

When a stock near its one-year high closes weak for a few days, it is usually profit-taking, not bad news. Buyers who missed the move step in, and the dip reverses.

Robustness

What failed, and what it taught me

  • A weak signal drowns under a heavy anchor. An intraday reversal paired with a heavy fundamental weight reached IR 0.04 and was rejected.
  • A fading edge will not survive new data. A rising-volume reversal looked fine overall (IR 0.111) but fell to 0.03 in the latest period.
  • Diversify by where, not just by formula. The same idea in an overlapping stock group was 0.64 correlated with the original, so it added nothing.
