How the brain encodes the rhythm of a smell

A machine-learning analysis of odor-frequency encoding in the piriform cortex. Co-authored with Prof. Debanjan Das Gupta.

95.3%accuracy decoding odor frequency from spike trains
1D CNNtrained on spatiotemporal firing activity
3kinds of perturbation test: ablation, shuffling, knockout
Co-authormodel design, ablation analysis, interpretation

The question

Odors reach the brain in pulses, and the frequency of those pulses carries information. The piriform cortex is the largest region of the olfactory cortex. The paper asks whether odor frequency can be read out from its neurons' firing, and which parts of the circuit carry that information.

What I did

  • Built the decoder. I designed a 1D convolutional neural network that takes the spatiotemporal firing activity of cortical neurons and classifies the odor frequency. It reached 95.3% accuracy.
  • Took the circuit apart. To find what drives frequency selectivity, I ran three kinds of test and watched how the decoder's accuracy changed:
    • Synaptic ablation removes individual synapses to test their role.
    • Time shuffling scrambles spike timing to test whether temporal structure matters.
    • Neuron knockout removes groups of neurons to test which sub-populations matter.
  • Interpreted the results with my co-author.

What we found

Frequency encoding showed statistically significant pre- and post-stimulus dynamics, and the perturbation tests separated the roles of individual synapses, spike timing and neuronal sub-populations.

Why it matters for engineering

The habit carries straight into engineering: build a model that works, then break it on purpose to learn what it really depends on. Ablation is how you find out whether a model has learned the signal or a shortcut.
